SIPLX Privacy Policy

What SIPLX stores, what leaves your device, and how to remove it.

Last Updated: 11 October 2026

SIPLX is a text-expansion app. Snippets you write are saved on your device and work without an account. Two features send data off your device. AI Format works on one snippet at a time, each time you ask for it. Cloud sync uploads your snippet library continuously, and there is no switch for it: it starts by itself once you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan. Staying signed out is what keeps everything on your device.

If you do not sign in, nothing is uploaded by cloud sync. SIPLX is fully usable as a guest: your snippets, folders and triggers stay on your device and are never sent to us. Using SIPLX as a guest creates no account with us at all — there is nothing on our side to look up, export or delete, unless you choose to leave an email address for product update emails (see section 2).

1. The Accessibility Service

Expanding a trigger into full text in another app is not something an ordinary Android app can do. To type for you, SIPLX uses an Accessibility Service — a permission Android grants only when you turn it on yourself, in your phone's system settings. It is off until you do, and turning it off again stops expansion immediately and does nothing else to your data.

What it can see

While it is on, the service receives events from the text field you are currently typing in: that the text changed, that a field gained focus, and that you moved to a different screen. To decide whether what you just typed is one of your triggers, it reads the text immediately before your cursor.

What it does with what it sees

Nothing you type in other apps is stored, logged, or sent anywhere. The comparison happens on your device, in memory, against your own snippet list. Text that does not match a trigger is discarded as soon as the check finishes. We never receive it, and it is not written to the app's database, to a file, or to any server — including while your library is being synced. Cloud sync uploads snippets you wrote in SIPLX, never text you typed elsewhere.

Where it deliberately does not look

  • Password and other sensitive fields are skipped. When the field you are typing in is marked by the app as a password, PIN, or similarly sensitive input, SIPLX drops the event without reading it and does not expand.
  • SIPLX does not read your keystrokes. The service does not request the key-event capability that would let it observe individual key presses. It works only from the text-changed events Android sends for the field in front of you.
  • SIPLX cannot control your device. It does not request gesture injection; it cannot tap, swipe, or navigate on your behalf. The only change it makes is replacing a trigger you typed with your own snippet text.
  • It does not read screens you are not typing in. The service acts on the focused editable field, and takes no interest in the rest of what is on screen.

Turning it off

Open your phone's Settings → Accessibility → SIPLX and switch it off, or use the shortcut in SIPLX's own Settings screen. Expansion stops; your snippets, folders and account are untouched. You can also pause expansion temporarily from the SIPLX notification without revoking the permission.

2. What we collect

Account information

Collected only if you create an account. An email address you leave only for update emails does not create one; see Product update emails (optional) below.

  • Email address
  • Display name
  • An account identifier we generate
  • How you sign in: an identifier Google gives us for your account. Continue with Google is the only way to sign in to SIPLX — we never receive or store your Google password. New accounts never set a password with us. A small number of older accounts still have a password on file from before September 2026, stored only as a cryptographic hash; that sign-in method has been retired, and those accounts now sign in with Google like everyone else.
  • A link to your Google profile picture, as Google supplies it with the sign-in. We store the link, never a copy of the image.

Snippet data — uploaded automatically while you are signed in

There is no cloud sync setting to turn on or off. While you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan, the following is uploaded on its own — when you return to the app, shortly after you save something, and when another of your devices changes something:

  • Snippet titles and body text — everything you have written in a snippet
  • Trigger phrases — the shortcuts you type to expand a snippet
  • Folder names and folder codes
  • Internal notes — the private note you can attach to a snippet. It is text you wrote, and it is uploaded with the snippet
  • Your AI Formats — the formats you create for AI Format: the name, description and category you gave each one, the instruction you wrote for it, the list of fields you asked it to produce, and which snippet uses which format
  • Change history — for each snippet we keep the fifty most recent changes, and each one holds the title and body text before and after that change, plus the name of the device that made it (on a phone, its maker and model; on Windows, the computer's name). This is what the app shows you as a snippet's history, and it means an earlier version of a snippet stays on our server after you have edited it away
  • Related details: snippet type, expansion mode, created and modified times, how many times a snippet has been used and when it was last used, and whether it is marked favourite, archived or deleted

On the free and Plus plans nothing you write is uploaded. Signing in on either of those still lets SIPLX download a cloud library you already have — your own data coming back to you — but nothing this device writes leaves it.

Attachments are never uploaded to us. Images, PDFs and any other files you attach to a snippet are not sent to our servers and do not appear on your other devices through cloud sync. They do sit inside the app's own storage, so Android's automatic backup includes them if you have device backup switched on in your Google account settings: that copy is held by Google under your own account, never by us, and your snippet database itself is excluded from it. Keeping attachments out of that backup as well is written but is not in a released version of SIPLX yet, and this section will be updated when it is. Moving to a new phone is a separate path: Android's device-to-device transfer carries your snippet database across, and today it does not carry attachments. To keep your own copy of them, use Export.

Device wake-up token — collected while you are signed in

So that a change you make on one device shows up on your others without you waiting for the app to check, each signed-in device is registered with us to receive a wake-up signal.

  • On Android the signal is delivered by Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service. Your phone asks Google for a registration token — an identifier for that one installation of the app — and we store it against your account so we know where to send the signal. The token is passed to Google each time we ask them to wake that device.
  • On Windows no third party is involved. The app holds an open connection to our own server and the signal arrives down it.

The signal carries no content. None of your snippet text, trigger phrases, folder names, email address or account name is in it. Google is told that a device should wake up; they are not told what changed, and there is nothing in the message for them to read. Nothing appears on your screen: the app wakes silently and then fetches the change from us over its own encrypted connection.

We store the token, which platform it belongs to, and when it was first registered and last updated. Registering the same installation again replaces the stored token rather than adding another. The token is deleted when you delete your account, and it is removed when Google tells us it has stopped working — which is what happens when the app is uninstalled or its data is cleared. Signing out stops this device sending or receiving anything, but does not by itself remove the token from our server.

Feedback you send us — collected only if you use it

If you answer the in-app “How’s your experience with SIPLX?” prompt, or send feedback from Settings:

  • The message you write, kept as you typed it
  • Which platform you sent it from (Android, Windows or the website) and your app version
  • How many snippets you had saved when you wrote it — a number only, so the message can be read in context

We link it to your account only so we do not ask again on your other devices, and so we can act on it if it names a problem specific to your account. So that a message reaches a person rather than sitting in a table, our server also forwards it to our own support address through an email delivery provider, Resend; the forwarded copy carries the message, the platform, the app version, the time it reached us, how many snippets you had saved, and which account sent it — identified there by its account id and by the email address on your account. Tapping “Rate on Google Play” from that prompt opens the Play Store in your browser or the Play app; we do not learn whether you went on to leave a rating there.

Product update emails (optional)

Only if you choose to leave your email address for them — on the website, in the Android app, or by ticking the box in the feedback form. We then keep:

  • The email address
  • The language you were using when you left it
  • Where you left it — the website, the Android app or the feedback form
  • Which version of the consent wording you agreed to, and when
  • If you were signed in at the time, a link to your SIPLX account

We use the address only to tell you about new versions of SIPLX and tips for using it. We do not send promotions to it, we do not sell it or share it, and we do not use it for advertising. It is stored with the rest of our data: in the database hosted by Neon, reached through our server on Render (see section 3).

To stop, use the unsubscribe link that every one of these emails carries, or write to siplx.app@gmail.com. After you unsubscribe we keep the address, marked as unsubscribed, only so that we do not email it again; we delete it when you ask us to. Deleting your SIPLX account deletes, at once, an address linked to that account — one you left while signed in to it. An address you left while signed out is not linked to any account, so deleting an account does not remove it: ask us to delete it instead.

Device information

Every request a signed-in app makes to us carries two small facts about the device making it, and we keep them:

  • A device name. On Android this is the phone's maker and model as Android reports them; on Windows it is the computer's own name. It is stored on the change-history rows described above, so the history can say which device made a change, and it is used for nothing else.
  • Which kind of device it is — phone or Windows — stored against the sign-in session. An account holds one phone session and one Windows session at a time; signing in again on the same kind of device ends the earlier session of that kind.

We do not collect an advertising identifier, a hardware serial number, your IMEI, your phone number, or a list of the other apps on your device.

Unsent editor drafts, on Windows

While you are editing a snippet in SIPLX for Windows, what you have typed but not yet saved is kept on that computer as an unsent draft, so that closing the editor does not lose it. A draft holds the snippet's title, its trigger phrases, its body text and its internal note, and it is stored as plain text in the app's own local storage on that computer. It is never uploaded.

A draft is removed as soon as you save or discard that edit, and drafts belonging to snippets that no longer exist are cleared automatically. Signing out does not remove them, and there is no control that clears them all at once. So text you organised with AI in the editor, and an internal note you typed there, can still be on that computer after you have signed out. A change to that is not in a released version of SIPLX yet, and this section will be updated when it is. Removing the app and its data removes them.

What we do not collect

  • We do not collect your location.
  • We do not collect your contacts, photo library, or files outside what you attach in the app.
  • We do not use advertising identifiers, and we do not sell data or use it for advertising.
  • We do not record what you type in other apps. Expansion happens on your device.

3. Where your data is stored

Cloud sync data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted by Neon, in Amazon Web Services' Asia Pacific (Singapore) region. Data is encrypted in transit using HTTPS.

The server our apps and our website talk to runs on Render, in its Singapore region. Everything sent to us passes through it: your email address and the sign-in that proves it is you, the token that renews your session afterwards, and your snippet data while you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan.

Our server also writes a diagnostic log — the ordinary record of what it did, kept so that failures can be traced. It is held by Render, separately from the database, and it is not reached by anything you delete: deleting a snippet, or your whole account, changes the database and does not go back into the log. Render keeps log lines for 7 days — the retention period for the plan our workspace is on — after which they are no longer available to us. We cannot shorten that period, and neither we nor you can erase one line before it expires. The log is not forwarded anywhere else: no third-party logging or analytics provider receives it.

Your email address has been written to that log. Several sign-in and account paths recorded the address of the account they were acting on, so that a line could be matched to an account. That changed on 4 October 2026: those paths now record an internal account number instead. Lines written before the change still carry the address. Either way, a line expires with every other line, within 7 days of being written — including lines about an account that has since been deleted. Deleting your account does not remove them; the 7 days does.

Neon and Render process this data on our behalf as hosting providers. Google processes device wake-up tokens on our behalf, as described in section 2. Resend delivers the feedback forward described in section 2, and so handles everything that copy carries — the message you sent, the email address on your account, and the rest. None of them is given your data for their own purposes, and we do not sell it to anyone.

4. AI Format

When you tap the AI Format button on a snippet, the text of that snippet and the format you picked are sent to our own server, which forwards them through OpenRouter — an AI gateway — to the language model that produces the formatted result. We choose that model on the server rather than in the app; today it is DeepSeek V3.2, and it may change. So the text of that one snippet is seen by OpenRouter and by the provider of the model in use. This happens only when you explicitly ask for it — never automatically, and never for snippets you have not chosen.

What is sent is the snippet's text and the format itself. A format is something you wrote, so the request also carries the instruction you gave that format, the list of fields you asked it to produce, and any rules you have taught it. Teaching a format by showing it examples is a second request, and that one carries the examples themselves — the before and after text you typed — along with any correction you wrote and the rules the format already holds. Attachments — images, PDFs and any other file you attach — are never sent to the AI.

Our server does not log or store the text you send, or the result that comes back from a successful call. When the AI provider answers with an error instead, the first 400 characters of its response are recorded in our diagnostic log so the failure can be traced. Beyond that it records only metadata about the call: its size, whether it succeeded, and the usage counted in section 5. Your account credentials are not sent to OpenRouter or to the provider of the model.

What a format remembers, and where

A format can be taught: you can give it rules, and you can show it examples — a before and an after that you type in yourself. On Android, what a format has learned is stored inside that format, so it is uploaded with the format and goes when you delete your account. On Windows it is held in the app's own database on that computer, is never uploaded, and is therefore not affected by deleting your account.

The Windows app additionally keeps, on that computer, the text of your fifty most recent AI Format runs, so that a run can be retried. The Android app keeps only how many characters each run was, not the text. Neither is uploaded.

What you can remove today, on Windows. The rules and examples you taught a format are cleared from that format's own learning screen, with Forget everything — one format at a time; there is no single control that clears all of them. The text of the fifty most recent runs cannot be removed from inside the published app at all: that control is written but is not in a released version yet, and this section will be updated when it is. Both are filed under the account that created them, so while you are signed out neither can be seen or cleared. Removing the app and its data removes everything it kept on that computer.

Purchases made on siplx.com by Windows users — subscriptions — are handled by Lemon Squeezy, LLC, the merchant of record for those sales. Which plans are on sale through each of them can change; the pricing page always shows what you can actually buy today. In both cases we never see or receive your card number, bank details or billing address. Google or Lemon Squeezy tells us only that a purchase happened, and our server then updates the Plus, Pro or Team entitlement on the account the purchase belongs to.

Lemon Squeezy sends our server notifications about a subscription (created, paid, cancelled, expired, refunded); we act only on notifications whose signature we have verified, and a repeated notification is not applied twice.

6. Team workspaces

If you join or create a team, content you place in that team's workspace is shared with the other members of that team.

  • Every member of a team can see and sync the full content of team snippets — body text, trigger phrases and folder — regardless of their role.
  • Team members see your display name. They do not see your email address. No team screen carries one, whatever the role of the person looking: not the member list, not the team statistics page, not the per-member breakdown an owner or an admin can open. Your own address is returned to you and to nobody else — on your own profile.
  • Until 2 October 2026 the team statistics page did send it. It carried every member's email address to every other member's device, and showed it in place of a display name that was blank, for any member of any team — there was no role gate on it. That was a defect, not a design. It was corrected on our server, which is what decides: from that date no app of any version is sent an address this way, and no update was needed for that to be true. A device that received an address before then may still hold its own copy on that device.
  • Please note that your display name may be derived from other information: if you signed in with Google, it is the name on your Google profile; if your account carries no name, it is the part of your email address before the @. You can change your display name to anything you are comfortable sharing.
  • The change history of a team snippet is open to the whole team. Any member, in any role, can open a team snippet's history and read its fifty most recent versions — the title and body text before and after each change — together with the name of the device that made it. A snippet's internal note is deliberately never written into that history, so turning a page of history does not reveal a note.
  • A snippet's internal note is not private inside a team. The note on a team snippet is always visible to the team's owner and its admins, and to an ordinary member once the owner grants that member permission to see notes — which is off until the owner turns it on. Notes on snippets in your personal workspace are yours alone.
  • Content already synced to another member's device cannot be recalled. Please do not put passwords, keys or private information in team snippets.

Team usage statistics — who used which team phrase

When you expand a snippet inside a team workspace, that expansion is recorded and uploaded to the team: the trigger phrase you typed, the title of the snippet it expanded, when you typed it, and how many characters it saved, attributed to your account. Which app you were typing in is not uploaded.

  • Every member of the team can see how much each member has used the team library — the number of expansions and when the last one was.
  • The team's owner and admins can additionally open a member and see the individual phrases that member used. Ordinary members cannot do this for anyone but themselves.
  • Expansions in your personal workspace are never uploaded. They stay on your device and no one else can see them — not the team owner, not us. Personal and team statistics are separate, and only the team half leaves the device.
  • We do not record the content that was inserted, only which trigger phrase was used. The content of a team snippet is already visible to the whole team.
  • This exists so that a team owner can check the work done with the shared library — for example, whether the prices quoted to a customer were the right ones. It is a record of use of the team's own material, not of anything you type elsewhere.
  • If you leave a team, records of what you did while you were a member remain with that team — they are that team’s record of work done with its own library. Expansions your device had not yet uploaded when your membership ended are discarded rather than kept. Two things remove the rest: deleting your account removes every event attributed to you, in every team — not at once, but with your account record itself, thirty days later, as described in section 7; and the owner dissolving the team removes that team’s entire history, for all its members.

7. Deleting your data

Individual snippets

Deleting a snippet puts it in a recycle bin rather than destroying it. For thirty days you can restore it, and for those thirty days its text still exists — on your device, and in your cloud library if it had reached your cloud library. When the thirty days are up, SIPLX for Android clears the title and the text and sends the emptied row to us, so the cloud copy is emptied too; what is left is an empty record that the snippet was deleted, which is what tells your other devices to delete it as well. That record is removed from the device after ninety days.

Delete forever, in the recycle bin, does all of that immediately instead of waiting: the text goes from the device and from the cloud in the same step. Use it if you want a snippet's text gone now. SIPLX for Windows has no thirty-day sweep of its own, so on an account you only ever use from Windows a deleted snippet's text stays in your cloud library until you delete it forever.

Your whole account

Open Settings → Profile → Delete account in the app. You will be asked to confirm who you are first, the same way you signed in — today that means Continue with Google.

You can also delete your account from the web, without installing the app: siplx.github.io/siplx-privacy/delete-account/.

When you delete your account you are asked whether to also delete the snippets you contributed to teams. If you choose to leave them, they remain available to those teams and are no longer associated with you.

After deletion, a de-identified record of the account remains for 30 days — without your email address, display name or sign-in details — so that the account cannot be signed into or re-registered in the meantime. After 30 days that record is removed as well, along with the purchase and Ink records described above.

All of that is the database. Our server’s diagnostic log is a separate store, and deleting your account does not rewrite it: lines written before the deletion, including any that carry your email address, stop being available after 7 days and cannot be removed sooner.

8. How long we keep things

  • Cloud sync data is kept while your account exists, so it can be delivered to your devices.
  • Deleting a snippet moves it to the recycle bin; its text is cleared after 30 days, or at once if you delete it forever. Deleting your account removes the content as described above.
  • Sign-in sessions expire on their own and are revoked when you sign out or delete your account.
  • An email address you left for update emails is kept while you are subscribed. After you unsubscribe it is kept only marked as unsubscribed, so it is not emailed again, until you ask us to delete it; deleting your account deletes it if it is linked to the account.
  • A de-identified record of a deleted account is kept for 30 days so the account cannot be re-registered in the meantime, then removed.
  • Our server’s diagnostic log is kept by Render for 7 days, then is no longer available to us. Deleting a snippet or an account does not reach it; it expires on its own.

9. The SIPLX website (siplx.com)

The sections above are about the SIPLX apps. The website is a separate thing and a much smaller one: it describes the product, it lets you sign in to the account you already have, and it lets you ask for update emails. Your snippets are never on it.

What the site keeps in your browser

The site sets no cookies, so there is no cookie banner to dismiss. Three values are kept in your browser's own storage:

siplx_refresh_token Kept while you are signed in, so that reloading the page does not sign you out. It is sent to our server to renew your session, and it is removed when you sign out. It has no expiry of its own — signing out is what clears it.
siplx_user_cache The account details our server returns when you sign in — your email address and display name among them — so the page can show who is signed in without asking again. Cleared when you sign out, and when you close the tab.
siplx-lang The interface language you picked. It is not tied to your account, and it stays until you clear your browser data. We do not send it to our own server, but it is passed to Google as the language for the sign-in button, at the moment you open the sign-in dialog.

Signing in on the website

The website only offers Continue with Google — there is no email and password form here. Choosing it loads Google's sign-in code, and only at the moment you open the sign-in dialog, not on an ordinary visit; Google sees that request. We receive an identifier for your Google account, never your Google password. If you are already signed in, opening the site quietly asks our server to renew your session, and asks it whether your subscription is still active — nothing else is sent.

Google Fonts

The main site loads its typefaces from Google Fonts as the page opens, so your IP address and basic browser details reach Google — before you sign in, and whether or not you ever do. This policy page and the refund page carry no such request: they use whatever fonts your device already has, and load nothing from Google. Google explains what it does with that in its own privacy policy.

What the site does not do

  • No analytics and no advertising trackers. There is no Google Analytics, no tag manager, and no product-analytics or session-recording tool of any kind on this site.
  • Nothing you type on the site is sent anywhere except to sign you in, the feedback you choose to send us from the feedback page, and the email address you leave for update emails if you choose to. The site has no snippet editor and no AI feature.
  • The download buttons take you to Google Play or to GitHub when you click them. Those are the only other places the site sends you.

10. Children

SIPLX is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we will remove it.

11. Your choices

  • Use SIPLX without an account. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Sign in on the free or Plus plan. Nothing you write is uploaded; SIPLX only fetches a cloud library you already have. There is no switch that stops a Pro or Team account from uploading — signing out is what stops it.
  • Use AI Format or not, on any plan. It runs only when you tap it.
  • Get update emails or not. None are sent unless you leave your address, and every one carries a link to stop them.
  • Export your snippets at any time from Import & Export, as CSV or JSON.
  • Delete your account from the app or from the web page linked above.
