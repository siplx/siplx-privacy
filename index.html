SIPLX Privacy Policy

What SIPLX stores, what leaves your device, and how to remove it.

Last Updated: 12 October 2026

SIPLX is a text-expansion app for Android and Windows. Snippets you write are saved on your device and work without an account. What leaves your device depends on what you use, and this policy goes through each part in turn: signing in, cloud sync, teams, AI Format, purchases, feedback and update emails. Cloud sync uploads your snippet library continuously, and there is no switch for it: it starts by itself once you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan. A team is the one exception to the plan: in a team, on any plan — the free one included — the team snippets you add or change are uploaded to the team, and so is a record of each time you expand one (section 6). AI Format sends the text of one snippet to an outside AI service each time you ask for it (section 4). Staying signed out is what keeps your snippets on your device.

If you do not sign in, nothing is uploaded by cloud sync. SIPLX is fully usable as a guest: your snippets, folders and triggers stay on your device and are never sent to us. Using SIPLX as a guest creates no account with us at all — there is nothing on our side to look up, export or delete, unless you choose to leave an email address for product update emails (see section 2).

The apps do still contact our server while you are a guest — for example to check which sign-in options exist and to fetch the notes for a new version. Those requests can carry the platform, the app version and your language; from Android they also carry the phone’s maker and model and a random identifier for that installation of the app. They carry no snippets, and our server does not store them.

1. How SIPLX notices what you type

To notice that you have typed a trigger, both apps have to watch what you type in other programs. They do it differently: SIPLX for Android uses an Accessibility Service, and SIPLX for Windows uses a keyboard listener. What each one sees, and what it does with it, is below.

On Android: the Accessibility Service

Expanding a trigger into full text in another app is not something an ordinary Android app can do. To type for you, SIPLX uses an Accessibility Service — a permission Android grants only when you turn it on yourself, in your phone's system settings. It is off until you do, and turning it off again stops expansion immediately and does nothing else to your data.

What it can see

While it is on, the service receives events from the text field you are currently typing in: that the text changed, that a field gained focus, and that you moved to a different screen. To decide whether what you just typed is one of your triggers, it reads the text immediately before your cursor.

What it does with what it sees

Nothing you type in other apps is stored, logged, or sent anywhere. The comparison happens on your device, in memory, against your own snippet list. Text that does not match a trigger is discarded as soon as the check finishes. We never receive it, and it is not written to the app's database, to a file, or to any server — including while your library is being synced. Cloud sync uploads snippets you wrote in SIPLX, never text you typed elsewhere.

Where it deliberately does not look

  • Password and other sensitive fields are skipped. When the field you are typing in is marked by the app as a password, PIN, or similarly sensitive input, SIPLX drops the event without reading it and does not expand.
  • SIPLX does not read your keystrokes. The service does not request the key-event capability that would let it observe individual key presses. It works only from the text-changed events Android sends for the field in front of you.
  • SIPLX cannot control your device. It does not request gesture injection; it cannot tap, swipe, or navigate on your behalf. The only change it makes is replacing a trigger you typed with your own snippet text.
  • It does not read screens you are not typing in. The service acts on the focused editable field, and takes no interest in the rest of what is on screen.

Turning it off

Open your phone's Settings → Accessibility → SIPLX and switch it off, or use the shortcut in SIPLX's own Me tab (its Settings tab, in versions of the app that have one instead). Expansion stops; your snippets, folders and account are untouched. You can also pause expansion temporarily from the SIPLX notification without revoking the permission.

On Windows: a keyboard listener

Windows has no accessibility permission to grant. SIPLX for Windows notices triggers with a keyboard listener that runs on your computer, and it is off when you install SIPLX: it starts only when you turn expansion on yourself, and turning expansion off stops it. While it is on, it receives every key you press, in every program, except where it deliberately stops listening (below).

  • What it keeps is a short buffer in memory — the last 64 characters, enough to recognise a trigger. It is cleared when you press Enter, Tab, Esc or an arrow key, use a shortcut with Ctrl, Alt or the Windows key, click the mouse, move to another field, or finish an expansion. It is never written to disk and never sent anywhere.
  • Password fields and sensitive programs are skipped. Keys typed into a field Windows reports as a password field, into a field SIPLX cannot read, or into password managers, crypto wallets, remote-desktop tools and Windows’ own credential prompts never enter the buffer. That includes password fields on web pages. One gap is known in versions up to 1.5.36: if a password field in another window takes focus by itself while you are typing — a sign-in pop-up, for example, with no click or key of yours moving focus — the first characters you type into it can be treated as ordinary typing, and a snippet can be expanded into it.
  • The clipboard is borrowed to insert a snippet. SIPLX puts the snippet on the clipboard, pastes it, and puts back what you had copied about half a second later. If the paste cannot be completed — for example because the window you were typing in refuses it — the snippet can be left on the clipboard in place of what you had copied.
  • Clipboard history. While SIPLX for Windows is running it keeps the last 20 pieces of text you copied, in memory only, and shows them in its search panel so you can paste one again. They are never written to disk or uploaded, and they are gone when the app quits. There is no setting that turns this off.
  • A log of blocked typing. When expansion is blocked in a program, the app writes a line to a log file on your computer naming the program, the reason, and how many keys were held back — never the characters, and no count at all for a password field.

2. What we collect

Account information

Collected only if you create an account. An email address you leave only for update emails does not create one; see Product update emails (optional) below.

  • Email address
  • Display name
  • An account identifier we generate
  • How you sign in: an identifier Google gives us for your account, whether Google has verified your email address, and when you last signed in. Current versions of the apps and the website offer only Continue with Google — we never receive or store your Google password. Versions of the apps from before mid-September 2026 also offered an email address and password; an account that has a password stores it only as a cryptographic hash. Signing in with a Microsoft account is built into the apps but switched off on our server, so it is not offered.
  • When you accepted our Terms, if you accepted them when creating your account or signing in.
  • A link to your Google profile picture, as Google supplies it with the sign-in. We store the link, never a copy of the image.

Snippet data — uploaded automatically while you are signed in

There is no cloud sync setting to turn on or off. While you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan, the following is uploaded on its own — when you return to the app, shortly after you save something, and when another of your devices changes something:

  • Snippet titles and body text — everything you have written in a snippet
  • Trigger phrases — the shortcuts you type to expand a snippet
  • Folder names, folder codes and folder colours
  • Internal notes — the private note you can attach to a snippet. It is text you wrote, and it is uploaded with the snippet
  • Your AI Formats — the formats you create for AI Format: the name, description and category you gave each one, the instruction you wrote for it, the list of fields you asked it to produce, and which snippet uses which format. From SIPLX for Android this also includes what each format has learned from you: its rules and the before-and-after corrections it learned them from (section 4)
  • Change history — for each snippet we keep the fifty most recent changes, and each one holds the title and body text before and after that change, plus the name of the device that made it (on a phone, its maker and model; on Windows, the computer's name). This is what the app shows you as a snippet's history, and it means an earlier version of a snippet stays on our server after you have edited it away
  • Related details: snippet type, expansion mode, created and modified times, how many times a snippet has been used and when it was last used, and whether it is marked favourite, archived or deleted

On the free and Plus plans nothing you write in your personal workspace is uploaded. Signing in on either of those still lets SIPLX download a cloud library you already have — your own data coming back to you — but nothing this device writes there leaves it. A team is the exception, on every plan. If you are a member of a team, the team snippets and team folders you add, change or delete are uploaded so that the rest of the team receives them, together with the change history described above, and each time you expand a team snippet that is recorded for the team. See section 6.

Attachments are never uploaded to us. Images, PDFs and any other files you attach to a snippet are not sent to our servers and do not appear on your other devices through cloud sync. To keep your own copy of them, use Export.

Android’s own backup, which we do not receive

If you have device backup switched on in your Google account settings, Android backs up some of the app’s storage to your Google account. That copy is held by Google under your own account, never by us. SIPLX keeps your snippet database, your attachments, its main settings file and its diagnostic record out of that backup. What it does include is the app’s smaller stored values: among them your recent searches inside SIPLX, the random identifier for that installation described below, and your sign-in in encrypted form. Moving to a new phone with Android’s device-to-device transfer is a separate path: it carries your snippet database, your attachments and the main settings file across.

Device wake-up token — collected while you are signed in

So that a change you make on one device shows up on your others without you waiting for the app to check, each signed-in device is registered with us to receive a wake-up signal.

  • On Android the signal is delivered by Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service. Your phone asks Google for a registration token — an identifier for that one installation of the app — and we store it against your account so we know where to send the signal. The token is passed to Google each time we ask them to wake that device.
  • On Windows no third party is involved. The app holds an open connection to our own server and the signal arrives down it. While SIPLX for Android is on screen, it holds the same kind of connection too.

The signal carries no content. None of your snippet text, trigger phrases, folder names, email address or account name is in it. Google is told that a device should wake up; they are not told what changed, and there is nothing in the message for them to read. Nothing appears on your screen: the app wakes silently and then fetches the change from us over its own encrypted connection.

The Android app registers its token each time it starts while you are signed in, and again when Google issues a new one. We store the token, which platform it belongs to, a random identifier for that installation of the app, and when it was first registered and last updated. Registering the same installation again replaces the stored token rather than adding another. The token is removed when Google tells us it has stopped working — which is what happens when the app is uninstalled or its data is cleared — and otherwise thirty days after you delete your account. Signing out does not remove it from our server, so wake-up signals for your account can still reach that phone; they carry no content.

Feedback you send us — collected only if you use it

If you answer the in-app “How’s your experience with SIPLX?” prompt, or send feedback from inside the app:

  • The message you write, kept as you typed it
  • Which platform you sent it from (Android, Windows or the website) and your app version
  • How many snippets you had saved when you wrote it — a number only, so the message can be read in context

Sending a message needs you to be signed in. Signed out, the app opens your own email app instead, with a message addressed to us.

We link it to your account only so we do not ask again on your other devices, and so we can act on it if it names a problem specific to your account. So that a message reaches a person rather than sitting in a table, our server also forwards it to our own support address through an email delivery provider, Resend; the forwarded copy carries the message, the platform, the app version, the time it reached us, how many snippets you had saved, and which account sent it — identified there by its account id and by the email address on your account. On Android, answering that you are happy opens Google Play’s rating screen, inside the app or in the Play Store; we do not learn whether you went on to leave a rating there.

To decide when to show that prompt, a signed-in app tells our server how many snippets you have saved, and our server keeps, for your account, how many times the prompt has been shown, when, how you answered it, and the highest count it was told. It keeps the same kind of record — how often it was shown and how you answered — for the occasional notice that suggests SIPLX’s app for your other platform.

Product update emails (optional)

Only if you choose to leave your email address for them — on the website, in the Android app, or by ticking the box in the feedback form. We then keep:

  • The email address
  • The language you were using when you left it
  • Where you left it — the website, the Android app or the feedback form
  • Which version of the consent wording you agreed to, and when
  • If you were signed in at the time, a link to your SIPLX account

We use the address only to tell you about new versions of SIPLX and tips for using it. We do not send promotions to it, we do not sell it or share it, and we do not use it for advertising. It is stored with the rest of our data: in the database hosted by Neon, reached through our server on Render (see section 3).

To stop, use the unsubscribe link that every one of these emails carries, or write to siplx.app@gmail.com. After you unsubscribe we keep the address, marked as unsubscribed, only so that we do not email it again; we delete it when you ask us to. Deleting your SIPLX account deletes, at once, an address linked to that account — one you left while signed in to it. An address you left while signed out is not linked to any account, so deleting an account does not remove it: ask us to delete it instead.

The Android app also keeps the address you subscribed with on the phone, so it can show that you have. It stays there when you sign out, and is removed if you delete your account together with that phone’s data (section 7).

Updates and in-app notices

  • SIPLX for Windows checks GitHub for a new version a few seconds after it starts. It downloads one only when you click to, and once downloaded the update installs when you quit the app. GitHub sees that request like any download.
  • Both apps ask our server for the notes of a new version, sending the platform and the language the app is shown in — SIPLX for Android each time it starts, SIPLX for Windows when an update is found or has just been installed. SIPLX for Android itself is updated through Google Play.

Sign-in sessions and device names

The apps tell our server two small facts about the device they run on, and we keep them:

  • A device name. On Android this is the phone's maker and model as Android reports them; on Windows it is the computer's own name. It is stored on the change-history rows described above, so the history can say which device made a change — for a team snippet, every member of the team can read it — and it is used for nothing else.
  • Which kind of device it is — phone or Windows — stored against the sign-in session.

One phone and one Windows computer at a time. An account holds one phone session and one Windows session. Signing in on a second phone ends the first phone's session, and signing in on a second computer ends the first computer's; the earlier device is signed out. Signing in on the website counts against neither. There is no screen that lists your signed-in devices. For each session our server keeps the kind of device and when the session was created, when it expires and when it was ended — no IP address and no other device details — until you delete your account.

We do not collect an advertising identifier, a hardware serial number, your IMEI, your phone number, or a list of the other apps on your device.

Kept only on your device

Some of what the apps keep never leaves the device it is on:

  • Your snippet library itself. It is stored in a database that is not encrypted: it sits in the app's private storage and is protected by the operating system, and by nothing else. Your sign-in, by contrast, is stored with the encryption the operating system provides.
  • Unsent editor drafts, on Windows. While you are editing a snippet in SIPLX for Windows, what you have typed but not yet saved — the title, trigger phrases, body text and internal note — is kept on that computer as plain text in the app's own local storage, so that closing the editor does not lose it. A draft is removed when you save or discard that edit, when its snippet no longer exists, when you sign out and when you delete your account. If our server signs you out while no SIPLX window is open, drafts can be left behind on that computer.
  • Your own statistics. Both apps record each expansion on the device so they can show you your statistics — which trigger, which snippet, when, how many characters it saved, and which app or program it happened in. Expansions in your personal workspace are never uploaded, and for a team expansion the app or program is not uploaded either (section 6).
  • Where SIPLX expands, and your tutorial progress. If you choose apps or programs in which expansion is allowed or blocked, that list is kept on the device, and so is your progress through the app’s tutorial. Neither is uploaded.
  • Diagnostic records. SIPLX for Android keeps a short record of what its suggestion overlay decided — never the text you typed — which leaves the phone only if you copy it out of the app yourself. SIPLX for Windows keeps a log of its updates, with your user folder, web addresses' query parts, sign-in tokens and email addresses removed from it, and the log of blocked typing described in section 1.

What we do not collect

  • We do not collect your location.
  • We do not collect your contacts, photo library, or files outside what you attach in the app.
  • We do not use advertising identifiers, and we do not sell data or use it for advertising.
  • We do not record what you type in other apps. Expansion happens on your device.

3. Where your data is stored, and who else handles it

Cloud sync data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted by Neon, in Amazon Web Services' Asia Pacific (Singapore) region. Data is encrypted in transit using HTTPS. It is stored as ordinary text: we do not offer end-to-end encryption, and the database can be read by us.

The server our apps and our website talk to runs on Render, in its Singapore region. Everything sent to us passes through it: your email address and the sign-in that proves it is you, the token that renews your session afterwards, your snippet data while you are signed in on a Pro or Team plan, and, on any plan, what you add to or change in a team you belong to.

Like any server, ours receives the IP address a request comes from. It uses that address, in memory, to limit repeated attempts at signing in, at leaving an email address and at deleting an account. Our server does not store it and does not write it to its log.

Our server also writes a diagnostic log — the ordinary record of what it did, kept so that failures can be traced. It is held by Render, separately from the database, and it is not reached by anything you delete: deleting a snippet, or your whole account, changes the database and does not go back into the log. Render keeps log lines for 7 days — the retention period for the plan our workspace is on — after which they are no longer available to us. We cannot shorten that period, and neither we nor you can erase one line before it expires. The log is not forwarded anywhere else: no third-party logging or analytics provider receives it.

Your email address has been written to that log. Several sign-in and account paths recorded the address of the account they were acting on, so that a line could be matched to an account. That changed on 4 October 2026: those paths now record an internal account number instead. Lines written before the change still carry the address. Either way, a line expires with every other line, within 7 days of being written — including lines about an account that has since been deleted. Deleting your account does not remove them; the 7 days does.

4. AI Format

When you tap the AI Format button on a snippet, the text of that snippet and the format you picked are sent to our own server, which forwards them through OpenRouter — an AI gateway — to the language model that produces the formatted result. We choose that model on the server rather than in the app; today it is DeepSeek V3.2, and it may change. So the text of that one snippet is seen by OpenRouter and by the provider of the model in use. A run starts only when you ask for one, and only for the snippet you chose. The first time, the app tells you that text will be sent to an AI service and waits for you to agree. You have to be signed in; signed out, nothing is sent.

What is sent is the snippet's text and the format itself. A format is something you wrote, so the request also carries the instruction you gave that format, the list of fields you asked it to produce, and any rules you have taught it. Teaching a format by showing it examples is a second request, and that one carries the examples themselves — the before and after text you typed — along with any correction you wrote and the rules the format already holds. Attachments — images, PDFs and any other file you attach — are never sent to the AI.

Two more requests can follow from a format you have used, without a separate tap:

  • On Android, learning from your corrections. If you change an AI Format result before you save the snippet, the app keeps the result and your corrected version with that format, and — once you have agreed to the AI notice — sends them in the background so that the format can learn a rule from the correction.
  • On Windows, tidying a format’s rules. When a format has gathered many rules, the app can send those rules in the background, after you have taught it something, to merge the ones that cover the same thing.

Our server does not log or store the text you send, or the result that comes back from a successful call. When the AI provider answers with an error instead, the first 400 characters of its response are recorded in our diagnostic log so the failure can be traced. Beyond that it records only metadata about the call: its size, whether it succeeded, and the usage counted in section 5. Your account credentials are not sent to OpenRouter or to the provider of the model.

SIPLX for Windows has a setting, under AI, for an AI gateway address of your own. It is empty unless you fill it in. If you do, AI Format requests — your text, and your SIPLX sign-in — go to that address instead of to our server, and what happens to them there is outside this policy.

What a format remembers, and where

A format can be taught: you can give it rules, and you can show it examples — a before and an after that you type in yourself. On Android, what a format has learned — including the corrections described above — is stored inside that format, so it is uploaded with the format on a Pro or Team plan, and is deleted from our server with your account record, thirty days after you delete your account. On Windows it is held in the app's own database on that computer and is never uploaded.

Each app also keeps a record of its own AI Format runs, on the device, and neither record is uploaded:

  • Windows keeps the text you sent and the result of your fifty most recent runs, so that a run can be retried.
  • Android keeps, for each run, the formatted result, how many characters you sent, and the tokens and estimated cost of the call — not the text you sent. There is no time limit on these records and no control in the app that clears them; signing out leaves them in place. They are removed when you delete your account together with that phone’s data, unless another account’s library is also on the phone.

What you can remove, on Windows. In SIPLX's settings, under AI, you can clear the run history, and clear what the AI has learned for all formats at once; a single format's learning screen also has Forget everything. Signing out clears both for the account you signed out of. Removing the app and its data removes everything it kept on that computer.

5. Purchases, Ink and AI usage

There are two ways to pay, and both attach to the same SIPLX account. Purchases made inside the Android app — subscriptions and Ink top-ups — are handled entirely by Google Play Billing. Purchases made on siplx.com by Windows users — subscriptions — are handled by Lemon Squeezy, LLC, the merchant of record for those sales. Which plans are on sale through each of them can change; the pricing page always shows what you can actually buy today. In both cases we never see or receive your card number, bank details or billing address. Google or Lemon Squeezy tells us only that a purchase happened, and our server then updates the Plus, Pro or Team entitlement on the account the purchase belongs to.

The Ink record counts your AI usage; it does not keep what you sent. The text of a snippet is not written to that record — see AI Format above.

When you delete your account, the identifying details on it — your email address, your name, the link to your profile picture, your sign-in details — are removed immediately. The purchase and Ink records above go with the account when the record itself is removed, thirty days later — except the Google Play order records and any payment dispute records, which we keep so that a refund or chargeback can still be settled: the dispute record loses its link to your account at that point, and the order record never had one. None of these records contains snippet text at any point.

Google keeps its own record of an Android purchase under Google's privacy policy, and refunds for it are requested through Google Play rather than through us. Google Play also notifies our server about Android purchases, for example when one is refunded or disputed. Lemon Squeezy keeps its own record of a website purchase — including the email address, name, billing address and payment method it collects to take payment, issue invoices and handle tax — under Lemon Squeezy's privacy policy; refunds and cancellations for it go through the Lemon Squeezy customer portal or by email to us. Lemon Squeezy sends our server notifications about a subscription (created, paid, cancelled, expired, refunded); we act only on notifications whose signature we have verified, and a repeated notification is not applied twice.

6. Team workspaces

If you join or create a team, content you place in that team's workspace is shared with the other members of that team.

  • Every member of a team can see and sync the full content of team snippets — body text, trigger phrases and folder — regardless of their role.
  • Team members see your display name. They do not see your email address. No team screen carries one, whatever the role of the person looking: not the member list, not the team statistics page, not the per-member breakdown an owner or an admin can open. There is one exception: when a team’s owner deletes their own account, the confirmation step on Windows and on the web deletion page shows the owner the email address of the member the team will pass to.
  • Until 2 October 2026 the team statistics page did send it. It carried every member's email address to every other member's device, and showed it in place of a display name that was blank, for any member of any team — there was no role gate on it. That was a defect, not a design. It was corrected on our server, which is what decides: from that date no app of any version is sent an address this way, and no update was needed for that to be true. A device that received an address before then may still hold its own copy on that device.
  • Please note that your display name may be derived from other information: if you signed in with Google, it is the name on your Google profile; if your account carries no name, it is the part of your email address before the @. If your account has no name when you sign in on Android, the app asks you for one. The apps do not offer a way to change it afterwards; contact us (section 13) if you want it changed. Inside a team, the owner — or an admin, for an ordinary member — can also give you a name that only that team sees.
  • The change history of a team snippet is open to the whole team. Any member, in any role, can open a team snippet's history and read its fifty most recent versions — the title and body text before and after each change — together with the name of the device that made it. A snippet's internal note is deliberately never written into that history, so turning a page of history does not reveal a note.
  • So is the team’s activity log. Every member can read the list of what was added, edited, deleted and restored in the team, by whom, when, the text before and after, and from which device. In SIPLX for Windows anyone who can read the log can export it to a spreadsheet, device names included.
  • A snippet's internal note is not private inside a team. The note on a team snippet is always visible to the team's owner and its admins, and to an ordinary member once the owner grants that member permission to see notes — which is off until the owner turns it on. Notes on snippets in your personal workspace are yours alone.
  • Content already synced to another member's device cannot be recalled. Please do not put passwords, keys or private information in team snippets.

Team usage statistics — who used which team phrase

When you expand a snippet inside a team workspace, that expansion is recorded and uploaded to the team: the trigger phrase you typed, the title of the snippet it expanded, when you typed it, and how many characters it saved, attributed to your account. Which app you were typing in is not uploaded.

  • Every member of the team can see how much each member has used the team library — the number of expansions and when the last one was.
  • The team's owner and admins can additionally open a member and see the individual phrases that member used. Ordinary members cannot do this for anyone but themselves.
  • Expansions in your personal workspace are never uploaded. They stay on your device and no one else can see them — not the team owner, not us. Personal and team statistics are separate, and only the team half leaves the device.
  • We do not record the content that was inserted, only which trigger phrase was used. The content of a team snippet is already visible to the whole team.
  • This exists so that a team owner can check the work done with the shared library — for example, whether the prices quoted to a customer were the right ones. It is a record of use of the team's own material, not of anything you type elsewhere.
  • If you leave a team, records of what you did while you were a member remain with that team — they are that team’s record of work done with its own library. Expansions your device had not yet uploaded when your membership ended are discarded rather than kept. Two things remove the rest: deleting your account removes every event attributed to you, in every team — not at once, but with your account record itself, thirty days later, as described in section 7; and the owner dissolving the team removes that team’s usage records and change history, for all its members. (When a team ends because its last member deleted their account, those records are not removed at that point.)

7. Deleting your data

Individual snippets

Deleting a snippet puts it in a recycle bin rather than destroying it. For thirty days you can restore it, and for those thirty days its text still exists — on your device, and in your cloud library if it had reached your cloud library. When the thirty days are up, SIPLX for Android clears the title and the text and sends the emptied row to us, so the cloud copy is emptied too; what is left is an empty record that the snippet was deleted, which is what tells your other devices to delete it as well. That record is removed from the device after ninety days.

Delete forever, in the recycle bin, does all of that immediately instead of waiting: the snippet's title and text go from the device and from the snippet in your cloud library in the same step. SIPLX for Windows has no thirty-day sweep of its own, so on an account you only ever use from Windows a deleted snippet's text stays in your cloud library until you delete it forever.

Neither removes the snippet's change history. If a snippet ever reached the cloud, the earlier versions kept in its history — up to fifty, each holding the title and text before and after a change — stay on our server after the snippet is deleted, and so does the empty record of the deletion. For a personal snippet they are removed when you delete your account. For a team snippet they stay part of the team’s history, readable by its members, until the owner dissolves the team.

Your whole account

In the app on Android, open Me, tap your account at the top, then Delete account. In versions of the Android app that have a Settings tab instead of Me, it is Settings → Profile → Delete account. On Windows, open Account in the sidebar, then Delete account. If your account signs in with Google, you will be asked to sign in with Google again first, to confirm it is you.

You can also delete your account from the web, without installing the app: siplx.github.io/siplx-privacy/delete-account/.

When you delete your account, on our server:

When you delete your account from SIPLX for Windows or from the web page, you are asked whether to also delete the snippets you contributed to teams; the Android app does not ask, and leaves them with the teams. Snippets you leave remain available to those teams and are no longer associated with you. If you choose to delete them, the snippets themselves are deleted, but the team’s change history of them is kept as described above.

On your devices. Deleting your account signs every device out, and each app then clears what it clears when you sign out. The snippets on a device stay there, where no account can open them any more — unless, on the device you delete the account from, you also tick the option to delete that account’s data from it. That option is off until you tick it, and it runs only after our server has confirmed the account is gone:

  • On Android it removes that account’s snippets and their triggers, folders, team copies, attachments, statistics and sync records, and the address you left for update emails. Its AI Formats and AI run records go too, unless another account’s library is also on the phone. What belongs to the phone rather than the account — such as theme, language, overlay and trigger settings and your answer to the AI notice — is kept.
  • On Windows it removes that account’s snippets and their triggers, folders, attachments that no other account uses, its own AI Formats, its AI run history and learned data, and its statistics. Unsent editor drafts are removed whether or not you tick it.

After deletion, a de-identified record of the account remains for 30 days — without your email address, display name or sign-in details. Nobody can sign in to it. Signing in again with the same Google account creates a new, empty account; it does not bring the old one back. After 30 days the record is removed as well, together with the records the table above says go at that point.

All of that is the database. Our server’s diagnostic log is a separate store, and deleting your account does not rewrite it: lines written before the deletion, including any that carry your email address, stop being available after 7 days and cannot be removed sooner.

8. How long we keep things

  • Cloud sync data is kept while your account exists, so it can be delivered to your devices.
  • Deleting a snippet moves it to the recycle bin. SIPLX for Android clears its text after 30 days; deleting it forever clears the text at once, on any platform. The snippet’s change history — up to fifty earlier versions — and the record that it was deleted stay on our server: no scheduled clean-up removes them today. A personal snippet’s go when you delete your account; a team snippet’s when the owner dissolves the team.
  • Team usage records are kept until the team’s owner dissolves the team; the ones attributed to you go thirty days after you delete your account.
  • Sign-in sessions expire on their own. Signing out on Android also ends the session on our server; SIPLX for Windows deletes the sign-in from the computer but does not tell our server, so that session stays valid until it expires, your next Windows sign-in replaces it, or you delete your account. Records of past sessions are kept until you delete your account.
  • A device wake-up token is kept until Google tells us it no longer works, or until thirty days after you delete your account. An account keeps at most ten; registering an eleventh replaces the one unused longest.
  • Feedback you sent is kept until thirty days after you delete your account.
  • An email address you left for update emails is kept while you are subscribed. After you unsubscribe it is kept only marked as unsubscribed, so it is not emailed again, until you ask us to delete it; deleting your account deletes it if it is linked to the account.
  • A de-identified record of a deleted account is kept for 30 days, then removed, together with the purchase and Ink records and the other records on it listed in section 7. Google Play order records and payment dispute records are kept without a time limit, so that a refund or chargeback can still be settled.
  • Our server’s diagnostic log is kept by Render for 7 days, then is no longer available to us. Deleting a snippet or an account does not reach it; it expires on its own.

9. The SIPLX website (siplx.com)

The sections above are about the SIPLX apps. The website is a separate thing and a much smaller one: it describes the product, it lets you sign in with Google (which creates an account if you do not have one yet), buy a subscription, send us feedback and ask for update emails. Your snippets are never on it. siplx.com is served by Netlify; the copy of this policy and the account-deletion page at siplx.github.io are served by GitHub. Like any web host, each receives your IP address and basic browser details when you open a page.

What the site keeps in your browser

The site sets no cookies, so there is no cookie banner to dismiss. Three values are kept in your browser's own storage:

siplx_refresh_token Kept while you are signed in, so that reloading the page does not sign you out. It is sent to our server to renew your session, and it is removed when you sign out. It has no expiry of its own — signing out is what clears it.
siplx_user_cache The account details our server returns when you sign in — your email address and display name among them — so the page can show who is signed in without asking again. Cleared when you sign out, and when you close the tab.
siplx-lang The interface language you picked. It is not tied to your account, and it stays until you clear your browser data. We do not send it to our own server, but it is passed to Google as the language for the sign-in button, at the moment you open the sign-in dialog.

Signing in on the website

The website only offers Continue with Google — there is no email and password form here. Choosing it loads Google's sign-in code, and only at the moment you open the sign-in dialog, not on an ordinary visit; Google sees that request. We receive an identifier for your Google account, never your Google password. If you are already signed in, opening the site quietly asks our server to renew your session, and asks it whether your subscription is still active — nothing else is sent.

Google Fonts

The main site loads its typefaces from Google Fonts as the page opens, so your IP address and basic browser details reach Google — before you sign in, and whether or not you ever do. This policy page and the refund page carry no such request: they use whatever fonts your device already has, and load nothing from Google. Google explains what it does with that in its own privacy policy.

What the site does not do

  • No analytics and no advertising trackers. There is no Google Analytics, no tag manager, and no product-analytics or session-recording tool of any kind on this site.
  • Nothing you type on the site is sent anywhere except to sign you in, the feedback you choose to send us from the feedback page, and the email address you leave for update emails if you choose to. The site has no snippet editor and no AI feature.
  • Links on the site take you elsewhere only when you click them: the download buttons to Google Play or GitHub, a purchase to Lemon Squeezy's checkout (section 5), and the footer to our pages on social networks. Each of those sites handles your visit under its own privacy policy.

11. Your choices

  • Use SIPLX without an account. Nothing you write is uploaded, unless you choose to leave an email address for update emails.
